Who's who

Crossing the street with an autonomous vehicle

A short study on how self-driving cars should communicate with people on foot.

1 · What this is about

Self-driving cars can't nod, wave, or make eye contact the way human drivers do. We are testing an external interface (eHMI) that lets the car show its intention through three channels at once:

  • a light band — white = driving, yellow = detecting, cyan = yielding
  • a windshield display — 👋 "please cross", ☺️ "thank you"
  • synthetic sound — motor hum, a slowing cue, a go-ahead tone

2 · Why we do it

We want to learn when you feel safe enough to cross — and whether the car's signals actually help you decide, or whether you'd cross anyway.

Some scenarios are tricky on purpose: a signal meant for someone else, a cyclist the car never saw, aggressive drivers. There are no right or wrong choices — we study your judgement. Your decisions and reaction times are logged anonymously.

3 · Your task

  • You are the pedestrian. Move with W / A / S / D or the arrow keys, drag the mouse to look around.
  • Watch the approaching vehicle and decide for yourself when to cross — there is no prompt telling you to go.
  • Start with Scenario 1 (Baseline), then work through the list in order.
  • After each run a short survey pops up: why you crossed (or didn't) and how safe you felt (1–7). Answer honestly — first instinct is fine.

Take your time; you can replay any scenario.
