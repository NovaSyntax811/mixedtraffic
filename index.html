Who's who

Crossing the street with an autonomous vehicle

A short study on how automated cars should communicate with people on foot.

1 · What this is about

Automated cars can't nod, wave, or make eye contact the way human drivers do. We are testing an external interface (eHMI) that lets the car show its intention through three channels at once:

  • a light band that is white when driving, yellow when detecting, cyan when yielding
  • a windshield display showing 👋 "please cross" or ☺️ "thank you"
  • synthetic sound: a motor hum, a slowing cue, a tone that says go ahead

2 · Why we do it

We want to learn when you feel safe enough to cross, and whether the car's signals actually help you decide, or whether you'd cross anyway.

There are no marked crossings here. When a car stops to let you cross, it is yielding by choice (prosocially), not because the law obliges it.

Some situations are busier than others. There are no right or wrong choices. We study your judgement. Your decisions and reaction times are logged anonymously.

3 · Your task

  • You are the pedestrian. Move with W / A / S / D or the arrow keys, drag the mouse to look around.
  • Watch the approaching vehicle and decide for yourself when to cross. There is no prompt telling you to go.
  • Pick a scenario from the ☰ menu, or the researcher starts one for you. Between situations you can walk around freely to get used to the controls.
  • After each run a short survey pops up: why you crossed (or didn't) and how safe you felt (1 to 7). Answer honestly, your first instinct is fine.

Take your time; you can replay any scenario.

What this was about

The car's signals were identical in every situation. What changed was the traffic around you: sometimes you were alone, sometimes other people crossed alongside you, and sometimes someone else had a claim on the road at the same time. In one situation the car had actually stopped for the person on the other side, not for you; in another a cyclist came past while the car was waiting. We are studying how readable the car's intention stays as the situation gets busier, and nothing here was a test of you.
